Research brief for operators, risk managers and brokers

Wounds are the claim you can actually control

Summary

Litigation is routine in post-acute and long-term care. A typical nursing home faces about one liability claim a year per 100 beds, and claims average $210,000 to $290,000. Pressure injuries are the second-largest driver after falls, and unlike many falls they come down to assessment and documentation.

How often facilities are sued

Benchmarks come from broker and actuarial studies measured per 100 occupied beds. Peer-reviewed counts per facility end in 2006.

Actuarial benchmarks put skilled nursing at about 0.8 to 1.1 claims per 100 occupied beds a year, at roughly $1,700 to $2,400 of liability cost per bed.12 In a large study of 1,465 homes, even the best-performing homes carried a 40% annual risk of being sued, against 47% for the worst.3 Good survey results alone do not insulate a facility.

For a 120-bed facility, that means about one claim a year and roughly $200,000 to $290,000 of expected annual liability cost. Severity is rising faster than frequency: one 2024 benchmark projects long-term care severity up 3.7% a year against frequency up 0.3%.1

Pressure injuries: second only to falls

Every major dataset ranks wounds and pressure injuries second only to falls, at 16% to 22% of claims and about a fifth of loss dollars.

LTACH and sub-acute care: high exposure, no public benchmark

Proxy, not a claims measure. Clinical pressure-injury rates stand in where public claims data does not exist.

No public study measures how often LTACHs or sub-acute units are sued. These settings take the sickest post-acute patients, and develop new or worsened stage 2–4 pressure injuries at 3.07% of LTCH stays, against 1.56% in inpatient rehabilitation and 1.23% in skilled nursing.5

Short-stay rehab produces large verdicts. Recent pressure-injury cases include a $15.75 million verdict in California (2026) after a missed stage 3 injury during post-stroke rehab, and a $13.2 million verdict in New Jersey (2020) over stage 4 ulcers during a 94-day stay. Several verdicts are under appeal or post-trial motions.

Carriers price these beds without a public benchmark, so documented wound-assessment controls give an underwriter something concrete to credit.

What it does to the insurance market

Skilled nursing liability rates are flat to +10% for 2026, with senior living professional liability forecast at +5% to +15% for 2027.67 Excess limits are thinning, and carriers are adding abuse sub-limits and punitive-damages exclusions. Buyers are advised to highlight strong risk management at renewal.

Where documentation changes the outcome

  • Admission. Wounds charted wrong or late at admission are where present-on-admission defenses fail.
  • The chart. The largest awards turned on charts that did not match the resident. Timestamped expert review closes that gap.
  • The submission. A documented daily wound review is a concrete control to put in front of an underwriter.
