Poster study, The Hospital for Special Care, New Britain, Connecticut

Evaluating the efficacy of a hybrid approach for wound management and risk prevention in a 236-bed long-term acute care hospital

Michael Seabock, RN, WCC, The Hospital for Special Care  ·  Rhett A. Gustafson, BA, WoundRounds®

Abstract

A large, high-acuity LTACH combined centralized clinical expertise with decentralized care-team access to a wound management solution. Over eight years, its full-thickness hospital-acquired pressure injury rate fell 70.2% between the four-year baseline and the most recent four years, and 78.9% in the most recent two years.

Introduction

Setting. Long-term acute care hospital, 236 beds, high acuity.

The Hospital for Special Care pairs centralized clinical expertise with decentralized care-team access to a wound management solution. The solution supports a culture of transparency, accountability and performance improvement, and reinforces patient safety.

It provides the technology foundation for wound and skin integrity processes: easy documentation, tracking and trending of wound data, and dashboards that show each patient’s risk drivers for potential harm.

Objective and methods

The study set out to show the benefits for care teams and clinical outcomes of linking a patient safety culture with a wound management solution over eight years.

Hospital-acquired pressure injury data from 2017 to 2024 was analyzed alongside facility risk data. The authors mapped wound and Braden Scale risk data against the patient safety protocols the facility introduced during the study period.

Results

Population risk. 58% of patients in the three highest Braden risk categories indicates a higher-risk population than typical.
  • The full-thickness HAPI rate improved by 70.2% from the four-year baseline to the most recent four years.
  • The full-thickness HAPI rate improved by 78.9% in the most recent two years.
  • 58% of the 236-patient census fell into the three highest-risk Braden Scale categories.

Conclusion

The facility raised its standards of care and improved wound outcomes by combining its patient safety culture and tested nursing protocols with a wound management system that supports care teams.
